# Python Experiments

Before moving everything into CUDA, I used [resources/fft_numba.py](../resources/fft_numba.py) to prototype the FFT pipeline and generate quick Matplotlib-based debug animations. That was substantially easier to inspect than debugging the same ideas inside CUDA kernels, and it helped validate padding, FFT multiplication, inverse transforms, and shift behavior before porting them to the GPU implementation. Below you can see an animation showing an instance of $\textit{Orbium Unicaudatus}$ evolving under the FFT-based update rule, and interacting with a wall obstacle. The top-right corner shows $|\hat{G}|$, the bottom left shows the shifted convolution result $G_{\mathrm{shift}}$, and the bottom-right corner shows just the growth function $H$ multiplied by $\Delta t$.

The controlled [six-arm walker](../hexapod.py) follows the supplied articulated-limb animation, with a hexagonal head blended into the torso and diffuse body and limb surfaces on a black background. Its reference-inspired density palette resembles the [Lenia showcase](../resources/lenia_showcase.gif): a warm pink and cream head nucleus surrounded by mint and cyan transitions, with blue-violet torso and arms. The head contains a pulsing warm hexagonal nucleus inside its hexagonal blue envelope. Both hexagons are one-third larger, with outer radius 32 pixels and inner radius 56/3 pixels; the enlarged head remains attached at its bottom side vertices. Three stacked chevrons form the torso, starting with the bottom edges of the head hexagon and repeating twice below it, with connecting side rails as in the original sketch. The two lower chevrons are 30% narrower than the head base, with their arm attachment vertices moved inward accordingly. Each mirrored arm pair attaches directly to the side vertices of its corresponding chevron. The torso is filled continuously between its chevrons and side rails, removing the internal gaps. The torso and arms remain predominantly diffuse blue-violet, with inner density waves adding stronger smooth blue-to-cyan and mint highlights along the arms and a continuous broad gradient across the filled torso, inspired by the control animation. Chevron outlines do not interrupt the torso shading. Warm inner tissue is confined to the head. The wake adapts the fluid renderer in [the fragment shader](../shaders/lenia.frag): silhouette density injects a persistent half-resolution field, state and fluid gradients generate curl, and the shader’s hash/value-noise functions provide curl turbulence. A bilinear backtrace transports this field; diffusion and exponential dissipation produce dark blue-violet wisps behind the body and moving arms. This standalone CPU adaptation preserves the walker geometry and motion; it does not turn the walker into a Lenia organism or reproduce the GPU shader exactly. The fluid freezes with the creature. Animated density waves travel along the limbs, and Gaussian smoothing softens the structures and their edges. Step circles are removed. It keeps six limbs, each with three articulated segments of 97.625 pixels, totaling 292.875 pixels. The third segment adds 50% to the arm length without shortening the existing segments. An arc-based inverse-kinematics solution distributes bending across both internal joints as the distance between root and planted foot changes. Resting footholds fan backward from the three attachment pairs at 0.8, 1.1, and 1.45 radians, 127.5 pixels from each root, leaving more room for joint rotation, resembling the seed silhouette. Each arm curves outward with visible bends between all three segments. The maximum reach follows the combined segment lengths. The server’s default preset now streams 50 larger copies through [the CUDA worker](../source/lenia/stream_hexapod.cu), with independent motion, sizes, palettes, and fluid fields, all updated and rendered on the GPU. In the CUDA gait, each arm independently randomizes its step threshold (150–250 pixels), swing duration (0.18–0.50 seconds), recovery delay, lateral reach, and extension margin after every stroke. Initial per-arm delays break synchronization while at least four feet remain planted. Each CUDA hexapod maintains a straight trajectory, reflecting its travel vector only at the hidden outer limit (`world radius - 8 + sprite diagonal radius`), after the entire sprite has left the visible circle. It does not continuously steer around or toward the center. Steering commands set a new inward or outward trajectory once; edge reflection then continues normally. CUDA body-density overlap accumulates injury and fades the body and trail to zero. The server replaces dead slots with new hexapods entering from outside the visible circle, resetting injury and fluid state and randomizing size and palette. The Python prototype remains only for standalone viewing and preview recording. Run `uv run --extra client python hexapod.py` from the project root to open its standalone pygame viewer; it does not need CUDA or the streaming server. Propulsion points away from the head while the six arms trail behind it. The default path curves gently in that direction; steering turns the head away from the target. Move the mouse to steer toward its position and press A to resume the automatic path. Space stops or restarts the motors, R resets the creature and automatic path, and Escape closes the viewer. Planted feet keep their world positions while the body moves; inverse kinematics bends the joints to maintain contact. Feet that fall behind their desired footholds reach forward over 0.24 seconds, with at most two feet moving at once. A 240-pixel step trigger lets the arms fold close to the body before reaching forward again. Forward footholds use one-quarter of the resting lateral spacing and extend along the direction of movement to 8 pixels inside maximum reach, making the extended arms nearly parallel to travel. Turns increase the reach margin by up to 80 pixels to preserve steering; the motor speed limit is 48 pixels per second. This gait covers more distance per step while keeping planted feet stationary. A simplified ground-contact resistance model converts backward stance-arm motor motion into body translation; without planted feet, the body cannot move. This is procedural walking with modeled traction, not Lenia evolution or a full rigid-body simulation. Stopping the motors freezes both the limbs and body. Display wrapping applies on both axes. Save the [CUDA GIF preview](../resources/hexapod/propulsion.gif) using `uv run python record_hexapod_cuda.py`; `--output` selects the destination, `--duration` accepts up to 30 seconds (default 8), and `--fps` accepts 5–60 (default 25). The native `--stream-hexapod-preview` mode follows one organism with the original reference palette and automatic path, without arena clipping. Motion, density, fluid, and color rendering use the same CUDA kernels as the server; Python only receives frames and encodes the GIF. The saved preview contains 200 frames at 1024×1024, with [sampled poses](../resources/hexapod/propulsion.png). The standalone prototype can still record using `uv run python hexapod.py --record output.gif`. Run `uv run python -m unittest discover -s tests -p test_hexapod.py -v` to check planted-foot stability, continued ground support, motor stopping, lack of propulsion without contacts, target steering, movement away from the head, frame-rate consistency, fixed limb lengths, bending at every internal joint, and density-driven wake persistence and freezing.

The earlier evolution experiment remains available as an [initial seed](../resources/hexapod/seed.png), [standalone catalog-format definition](../resources/hexapod/organism.csv), and [native runner](../hexapod_experiment.py), outside the active catalog and random births. Its limited [CPU parameter survey](../resources/hexapod/search-report.json) tested 54 broad and 96 narrower combinations using the existing polynomial Lenia rules without finding a stable six-arm swimmer. Native verification at `R = 13`, `mu = 0.15`, `sigma = 0.015`, scale 1, and `dt = 0.1` shows that the arms lose their structure within about 30 steps and the pattern subsequently spreads across the field; the [animation](../resources/hexapod/evolution.gif), [sampled frames](../resources/hexapod/evolution.png), and [mass measurements](../resources/hexapod/report.json) record that unsuccessful prototype. Run `uv run python hexapod_experiment.py` after building `build/lenia` in a CUDA/OpenGL desktop session to reproduce its 600 native steps. The runner temporarily appends the definition to a separate copy of the catalog and uses no steering or arena translation; its preview camera follows the center of mass. `--frames` accepts 1–1800 steps and `--output` changes the recording directory.
